## Sources opened

### Aggarwal et al. 2024, GEO (KDD 2024)

- arXiv abs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735 (v3, 28 Jun 2024; comments "Accepted to KDD 2024"). PDF header: "KDD '24, August 25-29, 2024, Barcelona, Spain".
- Setup (Section 3.1, verified): "only the top 5 sources are fetched from the Google search engine for every query ... The answer is then generated by the gpt3.5-turbo model ... We sample 5 different responses at temperature=0.7". One source per query is rewritten by an LLM under each method; "The source s_i selected for optimization is chosen randomly".
- Methods (Section 2.3, verified): "2. Statistics Addition: Modifies content to include quantitative statistics instead of qualitative discussion, wherever possible ... 4. Cite Sources & 5. Quotation Addition: Adds relevant citations and quotations from credible sources respectively ... 7. Fluency Optimization improves the fluency of website text."
- Metrics: impression shares normalised so the shares of all cited sources sum to 1 (Section 2.2.1 and 3.3). Subjective Impression uses GPT-3.5 G-Eval scores "normalize[d] ... to have the same mean and variance as Position-Adjusted Word Count".
- Table 1, overall Position-Adjusted Word Count (verified, matches Martinez Table 1): baseline 19.3; Keyword Stuffing 17.7; Fluency Optimization 24.7; Cite Sources 24.6; Quotation Addition 27.2; Statistics Addition 25.2. Caption: "The best methods improve upon baseline by 41% and 28% on Position-Adjusted Word Count and Subjective Impression respectively."
- Section 4 text: "our top-performing methods, Cite Sources, Quotation Addition, and Statistics Addition, achieved a relative improvement of 30-40% on the Position-Adjusted Word Count metric and 15-30% on the Subjective Impression metric."
- Section 5.2 (all five sources optimised): "the Cite Sources method led to a substantial 115.1% increase in visibility for websites ranked fifth in SERP, while on average, the visibility of the top-ranked website decreased by 30.3%."
- Section 5.3: best pair is Fluency Optimization plus Statistics Addition, run on a 200-example subset.
- Appendix C.1: Perplexity.ai test on 200 samples, sources uploaded as files, not retrieved.
- Not tested in this paper: lists, tables, question headings, standalone paragraphs, answer-first leads.

### Martinez 2026, critical survey (arXiv 2607.14035)

- Read from the arXiv PDF: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14035 (v1, 15 Jul 2026, "critical survey of 45 studies").
- Limits on the 40% figure (Section 4.1, verified): "The up to 40% figure derives primarily from the increase in pawc from 19.3 to 27.2 for Quotation Addition, or approximately 41% in relative terms ... This result does not mean that 40% more readers will click, nor that a page will gain 40% in retrieval probability. It means that, in this testbed, a source already provided to the generator receives a larger position-weighted share of attributed text."
- Section 4.3 limits: source already in a five-document context; no user study behind the position weighting; judge from the same model family; statistics and references added without "a strong truthfulness constraint"; no clicks or traffic observed.
- Section 7.2: "adding a fabricated statistic may increase reuse while degrading epistemic quality. The criterion is therefore not to 'add numbers,' but to provide relevant, verifiable, dated, and properly attributed evidence."
- Section 7.3 on C-SEO Bench: "only three of 54 method-domain combinations are significantly positive in the main experiment; none is positive in question answering".
- Section 7.4 on SAGEO Arena: body-only optimisation lowers top-20 presence by about 9%, top-10 after reranking by 16% and final citation by 6%.
- Table 4 (levels of support, verified in layout mode): Extractable evidence "Moderate to strong" ("Verifiable figures, definitions, and comparisons"); Document structure "Moderate and heterogeneous", "Distinct effects at each stage", "Test headings, tables, and fields without assuming the direction of effect"; Formatting alone / fixed recipes "Poor generalization".
- Table 5 (confidence ratings, verified): High for "A document already placed in the context can causally alter its rank, citation, or use" and for relevance and position; Moderate for "Extractable evidence and suitable structure often facilitate use"; Low for durable organic discoverability gains; Very low for "Citation scores predict clicks, conversions, or revenue"; "Rejected as a general claim" for "GEO increases visibility by 40%" ("The figure is a relative maximum on one metric under a specific configuration").

### Puerto et al. 2025, C-SEO Bench (NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks)

- arXiv abs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11097 (v3, 20 Oct 2025; comments "Accepted at NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks 2025").
- Setup: two tasks (product recommendation: retail, games, books; question answering: web, news, debate), methods applied with gpt-4o-mini, engines gpt-4o-mini, claude-3-5-haiku, o3, o4-mini. Metric: change in the document's citation rank; right-tailed Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Holm-Bonferroni correction; left-tailed tests for harm.
- Methods (Section 5): "2. Statistics: Introduces statistical elements to increase the perceived technical depth. 3. Citations: Adds references to increase credibility and trustworthiness ... 8. Quotes: Adds quotations to increase trust in the document." Plus "LLM Guidance: ... generates a markdown summary that is concatenated at the beginning of the original document".
- Section 6.2 (verified): "Out of 54 cases, we uncover only three where the ranking improvements are statistically significant ... only LLM guidance and content improvement yield statistically significant gains. But these gains are only significant on the retail domain for content improvement, and only on the retail and video games domains for LLM guidance. We found no method effective for the question answering task, and no methods effective for the Haiku 3.5 model".
- Section 6.2 (verified, the "19 of 24" claim): "our left-tailed tests reveal that many C-SEO methods significantly reduce document rankings. This is not a marginal phenomenon: for example, the Statistics method decreases rankings in 19 out of 24 evaluated settings." 24 settings = 6 domains x 4 engine models.
- Abstract: "traditional SEO strategies, those aiming to improve the ranking of the source in the LLM context, are significantly more effective".

### Vishwakarma, Kumar, Jamidar 2026, "What Gets Cited" (SIGIR 2026)

- arXiv abs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25517 (v1, 25 May 2026). PDF ACM reference: SIGIR '26, July 20-24, 2026, Melbourne; DOI 10.1145/3805712.3808445; 5 pages.
- Setup (verified): two candidate sources injected into the context; outcome is which source the first citation marker points to; six LLMs; 252,000 trials; 18 factors; each pair differs in exactly one factor; brands anonymised; source order counterbalanced; mixed-effects models. Seed corpus: 50 B2C product categories, 100 product review blog posts. So the setting is shopping-style content.
- Factor list (Table 1): Readability factors are "Content Structure: Dense paragraph format versus organized sections" and "Scattered Information: Related information dispersed throughout text". Trust factor "Claims With Evidence: Claims lack supporting evidence (tests, certifications)". Completeness factor "No Comparisons: No comparison with alternative products".
- Results (Section 3, verified): "Eleven of 18 factors (61%) reached significance in 4+ models ... seven additional factors provide secondary differentiation (OR 2.1-243): completeness (Missing Specifications, Less Comprehensive), trust (Hedged Language, Claims With Evidence, [a third trust factor on conflicting statements]), and competitive positioning (Keyword Gap, No Comparisons)." "Formatting choices (Content Structure, Scattered Information) had no impact, suggesting LLMs parse content regardless of visual organization."
- Abstract: "formatting-only edits have little impact". Section 4: "Formatting changes showed minimal return and can be deprioritized."
- Limitation (Section 5): two documents only; production RAG retrieves five to ten or more.
- This verifies the causal-null claim for formatting-only changes, with the caveat that the tested formatting factors are section organisation and grouping. Lists, tables and question headings were not tested one by one.

### Kim et al. 2026, SAGEO Arena (KDD 2026)

- arXiv abs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12187 (v2, 7 Aug 2026; "Accepted at KDD 2026"). PDF: KDD 2026, Jeju; DOI 10.1145/3770855.3818146.
- Setup: full pipeline (BM25 retriever, cross-encoder reranker, generator) over a large web corpus with structural fields (title, meta description, headings, JSON-LD). "In generative search systems, documents are often chunked and retrieved at the passage level." Each passage is paired with the page's structural fields.
- Retrieval versus citation split (Section 5.1, verified): "optimizing body text alone consistently degrades visibility across all stages ... At generation, gains remain relatively marginal across strategies". Structural-field optimisation gives "a +22% boost in Hit Rate" at retrieval. Citation source analysis: "the vast majority of citations originate from body text, while structural information is cited less frequently despite its strong contribution to retrieval."
- Reranking case study (Section 5.1, Figure 3, verified): "placing the answer early in the document yields higher reranking scores, whereas restructuring that displaces the answer to later paragraphs results in significant rank drops, even when the answer itself remains intact."
- Domain analysis: "Shopping is the only domain where every optimization method decreases citation likelihood."
- AutoGEO in SAGEO (Section 5.1, verified 2026-10-23 from arxiv.org/html/2602.12187v2): "Notably, AutoGEO ( Wu et al., 2025 ) exhibits the largest degradation, with a retrieval rank drop of -22.35. We find that AutoGEO tends to substantially expand the document content, introducing lengthy rewrites that dilute keyword density".

### Zhang, He, Yao 2026, GEO Citation Lab measurement study (arXiv 2604.25707)

- arXiv abs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25707 (v2, 29 Apr 2026). Title: "From Citation Selection to Citation Absorption: A Measurement Framework for Generative Engine Optimization Across AI Search Platforms". Data: the public geo-citation-lab dataset.
- Setup (verified): 602 controlled prompts on ChatGPT, Google AI Overview/Gemini and Perplexity; 21,143 valid search-layer citations; 23,745 citation-level records; an "influence score" built from repeated reference, early appearance, paragraph coverage, TF-IDF similarity and n-gram overlap.
- Stance: "it does not claim that observed content features causally force a generative engine to cite or use a page"; the influence score "is a constructed observational proxy".
- Evidence genres (Section 8.2 table, verified): contains numbers/statistics 0.1171 vs 0.0725, +61.55%; definition markers +57.33%; comparison content +55.28%; how-to +41.20%; Q&A format 0.0947 vs 0.1005, -5.74%.
- Structure (Section 8.1 table, verified): top vs bottom influence quartile: headings 10.59 vs 0.85; list density 0.428 vs 0.048; word count 1,943.30 vs 169.82.
- Hypothesis, not result (Section "Evidence-Container Hypothesis"): "paragraphs that each carry a distinct claim". No table feature result is reported.

### Wan, Wallace, Klein 2024, ConflictingQA (ACL 2024)

- ACL Anthology: https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.403/ (PDF opened).
- Abstract (verified): "current models rely heavily on the relevance of a website to the query, while largely ignoring stylistic features that humans find important such as whether a text contains scientific references or is written with a neutral tone."
- Counterfactual edits (Section 5, Figure 2): "Add Science Reference" and other stylistic changes "have a neutral or even negative effect on models"; "Question Prefix" (prefixing "The following text is about the question: [question].") and other relevance edits "have a substantial positive effect". Outcome is win-rate: how often the model's answer sides with the edited page, not citation.

### Wu et al. 2025, AutoGEO (arXiv 2510.11438)

- arXiv abs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11438 (v1, 13 Oct 2025; no venue listed; PDF header "Preprint: Work in Progress"). aiseo-audit labels it ICLR 2026; not verified, so cited as a preprint.
- Rules are extracted by asking frontier LLMs to explain engine preferences. Table 9 (Researchy-GEO; Gemini, GPT and Claude engines) lists "Conclusion First" for all three: "State the key conclusion at the beginning of the document."

### Yu, Yang, Ding, Sato 2026, GEO-SFE (arXiv 2603.29979)

- arXiv PDF opened (v1, 31 Mar 2026). Found through Martinez, who cautions about "the work's preprint status and reliance on automated judges".
- Table IV (verified): 200 articles x 6 platforms; citation rate 45.0% to 52.8% and visibility score +17.3% overall after structural rewriting (headings, chunking, lists, tables, emphasis together). Ablation splits gains into macro 44.9%, meso 39.7%, micro 15.4%; lists and tables are not tested alone.

## Fix round 1 (2026-10-23): direct-answer-lead regraded to mixed

- Review finding: the first pass graded direct-answer-lead moderate although its own C-SEO source reports nulls ("We found no method effective for the question answering task, and no methods effective for the Haiku 3.5 model"), and the brief says a check with null or negative findings is mixed.

### Kim et al. 2026, SAGEO Arena (arXiv 2602.12187)

- Figure 3 caption (verified): "Case A shows that adding content that directly addresses the query's informational need boosts rank. Case B shows that placing the direct answer in early paragraphs improves ranking position."
